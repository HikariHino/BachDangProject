# Gameplay engagement revision

The preparation chapter is now a short supply mission: one complete physical order at each of the four stalls, in any order. While preparing supplies, keys 1–4 select the next stall for the existing route guide; approaching any other stall still works. Timber keeps its directional sawing and splitting, rope keeps its four-point loop and timed tension release, rice uses repeated scoop-and-pour with an amount check, and provisions require collecting ingredients and folding four cloth edges. Existing vendor recordings acknowledge the work, with a brief head response on the nearby vendor. Completed work still supplies the persistent freight system and its one-time quality reward.

Native CLI playtests mute AudioListener before scene playback and throughout the test; they do not change saved game volume or system audio.

New work limits are 35 / 45 / 40 / 50 seconds for timber / rope / rice / provisions. Historical achievements keep their recorded time limits, so the shorter new missions do not retroactively downgrade old scores. Replays compare completion as a proportion of their own time limit.

At each sample stake, a second worker stands on a lower solid work tier connected to the boat by a ramp beside the player and holds the same timber. E calls for a 2.6-second brace; hammer releases still require alignment, appropriate power and the shared beat. The initial brace starts when the stake is erected. After all five physical sites, the player signals a floating test raft with E. Its leading hull is swept against the actual last sample's collider. The raft stops against that post, settles, and only then permits the days-later field montage. The trial and its camera are cleared on load/cancel; saving is unavailable during this transient action. Existing completed-field saves continue normally.

After the enemy begins pursuit, the player steers between two flags to choose a route. Green flags take the longer curved route (three passages, one arrow zone, 1.65 seconds of warning); amber flags take the shorter route (two passages, three arrow zones, 1.2 seconds of warning). Five volleys remain necessary. The chosen route and crossed passages persist in save data. Old battles already in pursuit or in the trap retain their progress without being sent back to new flags. The fleet still moves through its solid, separated lanes and follows the physical bait boat. Two close escort boats retain non-aggressive lure orders until the ambush.

The physical drum lesson now gives three visible responses: formation assembly, forward movement, then two front guards holding formation while two rear archers draw, release and hit real practice targets. Drill arrows cannot damage villagers or the player. Temporary weapons, bow props, targets and borrowed AI ownership are restored on exit/retry/load.

Short new ally calls explain the two river routes, warn of arrows, acknowledge the test raft and relay the drum orders. They use a separate low-volume field channel that waits for recorded enemy/commander speech and NPC dialogue. Clips are generated offline by Tools/prepare_field_calls.py using the previously approved Microsoft Vietnamese TTS service, then normalized and slightly accelerated without pitch shifting. No network voice request occurs while playing.

The gameplay HUD shares a dark jade and aged-gold frame: mission checklist upper right, concise direction upper center, chapter/tide upper left, health/stamina and hull lower left, medicine lower center, and contextual controls lower right. Frames use sliced runtime sprites, with no imported texture dependency. Borders, numerical hierarchy, track divisions and restrained opacity improve readability while leaving the center open.

QA: native Unity CLI engagement flow, historical rank timing, both route branches and save restoration, actual last-stake interaction and raft collision, physical drill arrows, non-overlapping HUD at multiple aspect ratios, preparation rewards and all four tabletop interaction paths. User campaign files are hashed before/after playtests and never used as test outputs.
